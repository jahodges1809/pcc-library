# Pentecostal Catholic Communion ### Library of Liturgical and Doctrinal Texts The **Pentecostal Catholic Communion (PCC)** is a sacramental Christian communion rooted in the historic faith of the Church, nourished by the catholic tradition, and renewed in the living presence of the Holy Spirit. This repository serves as the **authoritative digital library** of the PCC, containing its core liturgical, doctrinal, and constitutional texts. The PCC confesses **one Lord, one faith, one baptism**, and seeks to live visibly within the unity Christ has already won, while awaiting its full manifestation in the Kingdom of God. --- ## Purpose of This Library This library exists to: - preserve and make accessible the official liturgical texts of the PCC - provide clarity and transparency regarding PCC theology and practice - support prayer, worship, formation, and study - ensure stability and continuity of doctrine and rite Nothing contained here is secret or reserved. The PCC holds that the mysteries of God are **reverenced, not concealed**, and that clarity serves charity. --- ## Core Texts Contained Here This library includes the following primary texts of the PCC: - **The Missal** The Order of Mass for Sundays, weekdays, solemnities, and special celebrations, rooted in the Roman Rite and enriched by catholic and patristic tradition. - **The Breviary** The Daily Office of the Church, including Morning Prayer (Lauds), Evening Prayer (Vespers), Night Prayer (Compline), and associated psalmody and readings. - **The Rituale** Rites for sacramental and pastoral occasions, including initiation, reconciliation, blessing, anointing, and prayer at life’s thresholds. - **The Catechism** A doctrinal synthesis articulating the faith of the PCC in continuity with Scripture, the Creeds, the Fathers, and the living tradition of the Church. - **The Constitution** The foundational document outlining the identity, governance, authority, and mission of the PCC. - **The Lectionary (in development)** A structured cycle of readings for the Eucharist and the Office, drawing primarily from the Roman and historic Western lectionaries. --- ## Liturgical Character The worship of the PCC is **high catholic, sacramental, and reverent**, shaped by: - the Roman Rite and its historic forms - Anglican catholic ceremonial and discipline - patristic theology and symbolism - a pneumatological awareness of the Spirit’s present action The PCC values beauty, order, silence, chant, incense, posture, and symbol—not as ornament, but as theology enacted. --- ## Theological Orientation The PCC affirms: - the centrality of the **Eucharist** as the source and summit of Christian life - the **real presence** of Christ in the sacrament - the authority of **Scripture**, read within the living tradition of the Church - the witness of the **early Fathers** and the ecumenical Councils - the necessity of **hope**, grounded in Christ’s victory over death The PCC does not exist to win arguments or define itself against others. Its posture is one of **confident humility**, trusting that all truth ultimately rests in the Mystery of God. --- ## Authority and Reception The PCC understands authority as: - **received**, not seized - **relational**, not coercive - **pastoral**, not juridical alone Ministry within the PCC is exercised with the consent of the faithful and in continuity with the Church’s historic patterns, while remaining attentive to the Spirit’s guidance in the present age. --- ## Ecumenical Spirit The PCC recognises the work of God beyond its own boundaries and prays earnestly for the unity of all Christians. This library is offered openly, trusting that what is of God will endure, and what is not will fall away. --- ## Status of the Texts These texts are **living documents**. While they are treated with reverence and stability, they may be revised prayerfully as the PCC continues to discern its life and vocation. Major revisions will be documented clearly. --- ## A Final Word The Pentecostal Catholic Communion exists not as an end in itself, but as a **sign of hope**—that the Church may yet be visibly one, holy, catholic, and alive in the Spirit. > “Come, Holy Spirit.”
